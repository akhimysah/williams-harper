Buying in Morocco

The process, step by step

From reservation to handover of the keys: what a foreign buyer needs to know, in order, without jargon.

In six steps

Reserve, sign, receive the keys

Morocco allows a foreigner to freely acquire a titled property. The notary marks out the path, and our team accompanies you at every step, on site or remotely.

  1. The visit and choice of plot

    On the estate or by video call. You choose the villa, the plot, the orientation. Plans and the technical sheet are handed to you.

  2. The reservation

    A reservation contract fixes the plot and the price. A deposit, refundable under the terms of the contract, holds the villa while the preliminary contract is prepared.

  3. The preliminary contract at the notary

    The preliminary sale contract is signed before a notary. It sets out the specifications, the price, the payment schedule according to construction progress and the delivery date.

  4. Financing and currency

    The funds arrive in a convertible-dirham account opened in your name. This formality guarantees the repatriation of resale proceeds and rental income.

  5. Payment calls

    Payments follow the progress of the works, stage by stage, until delivery. You are informed at each phase, with photos.

  6. Delivery and title

    Keys are handed over after the acceptance visit, then your individual land title is registered with the Land Registry. The villa is yours, in the full sense of the word.

What you pay

One price, costs known in advance

Acquisition costs, registration duties, land registry and notary fees, amount to about 6 to 7% of the price. No agency fees: you buy directly from the developer.

The price is for a finished villa, with fitted kitchen, pool and garden delivered. The catalogue interiors are quoted separately.

Glossary

The words that sound scary

Eight terms you will read in the contracts, explained in one sentence.

Land title
The official document proving your ownership, kept by the Land Registry. Each villa on the estate has its own.
Land Registry
The administration that registers titles and mortgages. The equivalent of the cadastre and the property register.
Notary
A public officer who drafts and authenticates deeds of sale. They check the title, hold the funds in escrow and register the transaction.
Preliminary contract
The preliminary sale contract, signed before delivery. It binds both parties on the price, the specifications and the timetable.
Convertible dirham
A dirham bank account funded in foreign currency. It guarantees that you can transfer resale proceeds or rental income abroad.
Exchange Office
The body that regulates currency movements. Your transfers are declared to it by the bank, with no action on your part.
Payment call
Each interim payment, triggered by a construction stage: foundations, structural work, weather-tight shell, finishes.
Acceptance
The delivery visit, during which you check the villa room by room and note any reservations before the keys are handed over.

General information, current in 2026. Each situation is confirmed by the notary and, if needed, by our partner tax adviser before signing.
